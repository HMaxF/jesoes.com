What is the Will of GOD?

Understanding Faith Beyond Religious Performance

1. The Definition: What is the Will of God?

Jesus defines the "will of the Father" as a heart-level commitment rather than a checklist of chores.

  • To Believe: "For my Father’s will is that everyone who looks to the Son and believes in him shall have eternal life." (John 6:40).
  • To Love: Loving God with all your heart and loving your neighbor as yourself (Matthew 22:37-40).
  • To Repent: Turning away from self-will toward God's lead.
    * God desires a heart of mercy and justice rather than just external sacrifices or rule-following.

2. Is a Good Deed Mandatory to Stay Saved?

No, we don't "keep" our faith by routinely performing tasks.

Good deeds are the evidence of life, not the cause of it. Like a fruit tree, the fruit doesn't make the tree alive; the life in the tree produces the fruit.

Since we didn’t start our spiritual life by being "good," we are not maintained by our own efforts. We are held secure by Jesus’ finished work on the cross.

3. Example of People Doing Good Deeds but Rejected By Jesus

Matthew 7:21-23

21 "Not everyone who says to me, 'Lord, Lord,' will enter into the Kingdom of Heaven; but he who does the will of my Father who is in heaven."

22 "Many will tell me in that day, 'Lord, Lord, didn't we prophesy in your name, in your name cast out demons, and in your name do many mighty works?'"

23 "Then I will tell them, 'I never knew you. Depart from me, you who work iniquity.'"

Reason A: Intimacy over Industry

Jesus didn't say, "You didn't do enough." The key phrase is "I never knew you." These people were busy working for Jesus, but they never spent time being with Jesus. They had a "professional" relationship with religion, but no personal relationship with the Savior.

Reason B: Motive and Authority

They used Jesus' name as a tool for power or reputation. Their deeds were done for their own glory ("Look at what we did!") rather than out of humble obedience to God's lead.

Reason C: Hidden Lawlessness

While their public religious life was impressive, their private life was not surrendered to God's character. They lacked the "Fruit of the Spirit."

Jesus emphasizes Relationship over Performance. One can perform religious miracles but still remain a "practitioner of lawlessness" if their heart is not surrendered to Him.

5. Can I Fulfill the "Will of God" by living in solitude?

Because we are saved by God's grace and faith alone, and we are not mandated to "do good deeds," is it acceptable for a believer to live in permanent solitude?

While our salvation is secure through faith, a life in isolation often leads to a faith that "starves."

Mark 16:15

He said to them, "Go into all the world, and preach the Good News to the whole creation."

This "Great Commission" shows that believers are called to engage with others, sharing the message of salvation and living out their faith in community.

Matthew 5:14

"You are the light of the world. A city located on a hill can't be hidden."

A light bulb sitting alone in a box is safe, but it isn't fulfilling its purpose. It was made to be plugged into a socket to light up a room for others.

Verdict: While living in isolation doesn't "cancel" our salvation, it can "limit" our joy and growth. True faith is like a seed; it is saved by the soil of Grace, but it is designed to grow upward and outward to provide shade and fruit for others.
